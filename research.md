# Research

<p class="lede">Stochastic path integrals, weak-noise asymptotics, and the complex saddles that control escape rates.</p>

The path integral gives a third route to stochastic processes alongside the Langevin SDE and the Fokker–Planck PDE. In the weak-noise limit $D \to 0$ it is dominated by stationary paths of the Onsager–Machlup functional, and there is a beautiful correspondence between the most probable stochastic paths and Hamiltonian mechanics in an effective potential. My work shows that in one of the most basic problems, escape from a metastable well, the dominant paths leave the real line, and that Picard–Lefschetz theory is the right framework for making sense of them.

## Papers

<div class="entry">
  <h3><a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1751-8121/ae8a00">Complex paths for real stochastic processes</a></h3>
  <div class="meta">D. A. Baldwin, A. J. McKane, S. P. Fitzgerald · <em>J. Phys. A: Math. Theor.</em>, 2026</div>
  <p>Previous derivations of the decay rate of a metastable state in the path-integral formalism relied on a step that is difficult to justify mathematically. We show the difficulty is resolved by working with an extremal solution that arises naturally in the Itô formulation: a complex bounce. A simple cubic potential is chosen so that the solution can be written in elementary functions.</p>

## Thesis

<div class="entry">
  <h3><a href="https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/38065/">Complex saddles in stochastic path integrals</a></h3>
  <div class="meta">PhD thesis · University of Leeds, 2025 · Supervisor: Prof. Steve Fitzgerald</div>
  <p>Picard–Lefschetz theory as a rigorous framework for evaluating stochastic path integrals in the weak-noise limit. The integral over the quasi-zero-mode direction, which diverges for real instanton–anti-instanton configurations, becomes convergent on the appropriate thimble. Introduces the <em>stochastic complex bounce</em>, an algorithm incorporating quadratic fluctuations about complex saddles, and escape rates for the stochastic cubic and sine-Gordon potentials.</p>
